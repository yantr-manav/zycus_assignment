# Design Notes — Bookable Payable AI

## 1. What I understood later that I did not on day one

Day one, this looks like OCR-plus-field-mapping. It is not. The ERP does not book what the page *shows*; it books what its own arithmetic *derives* from the raw components you submit. The task is a translation from the document's presentation to the ERP's model, and almost every hard document is one translation hiding behind several masks:

- **Tax-inclusive pricing** (“Unit Price (incl VAT)”, “P.VENDA C/IVA”): the printed price is the taxed amount. The ERP adds tax on top of what you give it, so a faithful copy overstates the gross. The translation is a net base per taxable line (`incl ÷ (1+rate)`), with zero-rated items untouched — and the printed tax total is the arbiter of which items are zero-rated.
- **Pack and price units** (“Price unit 100”, qty 100 PC, amount 771.66): the ERP multiplies quantity × unit price, so the faithful copy books 100× too much. The translation is to express the line in price-units (the document itself says the price covers a pack).
- **Tax represented once.** A rate in a line column *and* a totals-block tax amount is how most invoices present tax; submitting both levels charges it twice, because the ERP sums line and header taxes independently. One placement, the document's own printed amount, is the faithful model.
- **Discounts represented once** (line percentage XOR totals-block deduction XOR negative row), **credits with positive magnitudes** plus `CREDIT_MEMO`, **withholding as a negative tax**, **duties as bookable rows** (quantity 1 × printed amount when no unit price exists), **locale numbers** (`24,000` is 24.00 on a Portuguese invoice; the row arithmetic proves it).
- And the quiet one: **a document set is not a document**. A dossier is freight invoice + goods invoice + customs permit + waybill; the permits and waybills are not payables, the two invoices are two payables, and a page from a different seller with a price and a total is its own payable even without the word “invoice”.

Each mask looked like a special case until it became the same sentence: *presentation ≠ the ERP's model, and the builder's job is the translation.*

## 2. What the system does on a document unlike any it has seen

Nothing about the machinery branches on filename, supplier, or a list of known cases. The pipeline:

1. **Classifies before extracting** — zero, one, or several payables, and declines for proformas, permits, statements, dunning letters. Supporting pages do not make a set non-payable; the governing payable is still found.
2. **Extracts raw components under translation rules** that describe the ERP's finite computation and the presentation patterns above — not answers. The model may return an empty field; it may not invent a value to make a total foot.
3. **Applies deterministic arithmetic** where the document makes it certain: a quantity uniquely pinned by printed row arithmetic is restored (`total ÷ (price × (1−discount))`, only when exact and not explained by a tax rate); totals and tax declarations are checked against components.
4. **Reconciles through the sealed calculator** and, when it disagrees, runs a **bounded signal→repair loop**: the system states facts (“the ERP derives X on this line; the page prints Y”; “the declared tax is Z, the components contribute W”), and the model proposes source-quoted corrections. Deterministic code accepts a change only if it is grounded, schema-clean and provably better — or, for a removal, lands exactly on the printed amount.

That is why it generalises instead of guessing: the mechanisms are keyed to the ERP's model and to *provable* arithmetic on the page, not to the documents in the folder. The measured record is consistent with that: every payable in the candidate set recomputes to the amount its document states — including the documents built to punish surface-level readings (pack-scaled prices, tax-inclusive pricing, duplicated tax levels, a multi-invoice shipment dossier, a document with a row printed only on a section label line, and documents whose credit/withholding signs flip the expected direction).

## 3. A document that could not be solved the way the others were

`HLD-05.pdf` (a Portuguese *Fatura* that embeds an IEC excise inside its prices and repeats its VAT as both per-line columns and a totals-block analysis). The tempting reading — infer a per-line excise duty or rate the page never states, so the pieces foot to the printed total — is precisely the plug the brief forbids, and the first attempts stalled exactly there: a beautiful narrative (`the excise must be the difference`) over numbers the document does not print. Two independent facts settled it. First, the totals block itself prints the taxable base (835.27), each rate's tax (19.82 at 13%, 157.05 at 23%), the IEC excise total (88.33) and the untaxed container deposit (25.80), so nothing about the excise must be inferred — the translation is to submit the source's own quantities, unit prices and discount percentages, the printed taxes once at the header, the printed excise in `excise_duties`, and the deposits as untaxed lines; that reconstruction is **proven to book 1,037.94 exactly** against the unchanged ERP by a regression test. Second, the same document demonstrates the safe failure mode: a representation that places the same rates at both tax levels double-charges them, and the deterministic de-duplication is permitted only because clearing the copies reproduces the printed gross *exactly* — never because a narrative said so. The same discipline decides the closest call in the set, `INV-26.pdf`: a 35-row Malaysian sales invoice where one row's amount (27.40) is printed only alongside a section label. Two independent high-magnification reads of the page missed that row and could not foot the table; the correct response was to keep hunting for the printed fact rather than adjust a number to close the 27.40 gap — the strong vision pass found the row, and every row of the document now sums exactly to its printed total. Distinguishing “the document doesn't say” from “I haven't found where it says it” is the same discipline that keeps a matching gross from being treated as proof of a correct record.

## 4. Limits and safe operation

The translation of a tax-inclusive or pack-scaled line is a model task; the deterministic layer verifies it (schema, grounding, exact reconciliation) but cannot manufacture it. Weak or free-tier readers can mis-transcribe dense tables — the line-sum completeness signal catches exactly that class (extracted rows that reproduce no printed total) and drives one corrective re-read, but it cannot conjure fidelity from a reader that cannot see the page; the strongest vision model available should be used for dense scans. OCR can garble a quote even when the page is legible, in which case repairs fail closed and the residual is flagged rather than hidden. The included tests validate the code paths and invariants (resolver exactness, grounding, clear-op exactness, loop convergence, completeness signal, chunk splitting/merging, key rotation and redaction, provider error classification); they are not a substitute for reviewing `logs/run.jsonl` and the source PDFs, which the workflow requires.
